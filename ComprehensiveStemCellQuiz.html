1. Which two fundamental properties define a true stem cell?
Hint: Think about what makes a stem cell dual-purposed: it must maintain its own reservoir in the body while also being able to generate functionally mature progeny.
Correct! Stem cells are defined by their unique ability to undergo self-renewal (making identical copies of themselves) and to differentiate into various functional, specialized cell types.

2. At what developmental stage are mammalian cells considered strictly totipotent, capable of forming both embryonic and extraembryonic tissues?
Hint: This potency level represents the absolute maximum developmental versatility, allowing the creation of a complete organism plus its survival structures in the womb.
Correct! Totipotency represents the highest level of developmental potential, and only the zygote and very early blastomeres possess the ability to form both the entire embryo and the supporting extraembryonic tissues (like the placenta).
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
3. Embryonic stem cells (ESCs) derived from the inner cell mass of a blastocyst are classified as:
Hint: Compare these cells to the zygote; remember that while they can build almost any tissue in the body, they cannot build the placenta.
Correct! Pluripotent stem cells can differentiate into derivatives of the ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm, but they cannot independently form a whole functional organism because they lack extraembryonic potency.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
4. Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) found in the bone marrow are a classic example of which level of potency?
Hint: Consider how adult bone marrow cells can produce red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets, but not skin or neurons.
Correct! Multipotent stem cells have a more limited repertoire, giving rise to multiple specialized cell types within a specific lineage or tissue system, such as myeloid and lymphoid blood cells.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
5. During gastrulation, the single-layered blastula is reorganized into a trilaminar structure consisting of the ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm. Which of the following derivative pairings is correct?
Hint: Recall which layer covers the outside of the body and forms the brain (ecto-), versus the layer that lines the internal tube of the digestive tract (endo-).
Correct! The ectoderm forms external and neural structures, the mesoderm forms muscle, bone, and circulatory systems, and the endoderm forms internal visceral linings like the gut and lungs.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
6. How does asymmetric cell division allow a stem cell population to maintain tissue homeostasis?
Hint: Think about the quantitative balance: one cell stays in the stem compartment, and the other leaves it to become functional tissue.
Correct! Asymmetric division yields two distinct fates: one daughter cell preserves the stem cell identity, while the other begins specialized differentiation to replace lost or damaged cells.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
7. Although all somatic cells in an organism contain essentially the same genomic DNA, different cell types express vastly different proteins. What primary molecular mechanism drives this specialization from stem cells?
Hint: Consider how the book of life (DNA) remains the same in every cell, but cells use bookmarks and locks (epigenetics) to read only specific chapters.
Correct! Cell specialization is controlled by selectively activating or silencing specific sets of genes through DNA methylation, histone modification, and cell-specific transcription factors without altering the underlying DNA sequence.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
8. Morphogens are signaling molecules that direct stem cell differentiation in a concentration-dependent manner. A classic example of a morphogen gradient establishing positional identity in development is:
Hint: Think of a famous signaling protein named after a fast video game character that patterns digits on vertebrate limbs and the neural tube.
Correct! Sonic Hedgehog acts as a morphogen forming spatial concentration gradients that pattern neural tube development, limb polarity, and various other organogenesis processes.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
9. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are generated from adult somatic cells (such as fibroblasts) by the introduction of the Yamanaka factors. Which set of transcription factors comprises the core Yamanaka cocktail?
Hint: This quartet of transcription factors includes two key master regulators of pluripotency (Oct4 and Sox2) alongside an oncogene (c-Myc) and a growth factor regulator (Klf4).
Correct! Shinya Yamanaka demonstrated that introducing these four transcription factors into differentiated somatic cells can reprogram them back into an embryonic-like pluripotent state.
Incorrect. Please check the hint and try again!
10. A researcher isolates a multipotent stem cell from adult adipose tissue. If cultured under specific cytokine conditions, which limitation will this cell inherently possess compared to an embryonic stem cell?
Hint: Revisit the distinction between multipotency and pluripotency regarding how broad of a lineage spectrum a stem cell can access.
Correct! Because it is a multipotent adult stem cell, it cannot generate cells belonging to all three germ layers (e.g., it cannot form neural or endodermal gut tissues) the way a pluripotent embryonic stem cell can.
